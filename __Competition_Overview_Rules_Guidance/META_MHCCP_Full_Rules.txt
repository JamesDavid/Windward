5. ENTRY REQUIREMENTS
Each Entry must meet all of the following requirements, as determined by Sponsor in its sole discretion, or the Entry may be disqualified:

 

Submission Requirements

The prototype must implement a complete, genuinely playable core loop: a repeatable cycle of player actions with real-time feedback, a clear goal and a win, lose, or reset state, and one or more forms of meaningful progression within a single play session. It must be playable and repeatable, not a static mockup or click-through.
The prototype must be built using AI tools (prompt-built), and a build log must be submitted with the entry.
The prototype must be single-player and built for portrait orientation. Winning prototypes are rebuilt as single-player, portrait games on the Early Access Creation Tools, and these constraints also form part of the Completion Checklist (Addendum A, Section 4).
The submission must be original to the Entrant. The Entrant may build on their own prior Meta Horizon Creator Competition design, but the prototype may not be a game already published or live on the platform, and may not replicate an existing game. All content must be original or use assets the Entrant has rights to.
The game prototype must fall within one of the following genres, as further described on the Devpost page at https://mhcp-game-prototype.devpost.com/: (1) Survival & Resource Management; (2) Simulation & Management; or (3) Tower Defense & Strategy. Entrants must select one genre at the time of submission.
Any text in the submission, including the design-intent document, must be in English.
 

Submission Fields on Devpost

All required items added to the Submission of the Entry on Devpost which includes:

The playable prototype build. Submit your prototype as a self-contained Three.js / HTML5 build. It must meet all of the following:
Package. A single .zip file, no larger than 35MB, with index.html at the top level of the .zip and not inside a folder.
Your game code. Your submitted index.html must contain all of your own game code, in readable, unminified form. You may develop across as many source files as you like, but you must assemble them into a single index.html before you submit.
Third-party libraries. Libraries such as Three.js must be included in the .zip inside a folder named vendor, and referenced with relative paths. Do not embed them in index.html.
Everything else your game needs. All assets, fonts, images, audio and data must be included in the .zip and referenced with relative paths.
No network access. The build must not make any external network request while it is running.
Builds that load resources from external URLs, including content delivery networks (CDNs), will fail validation.

A design-intent document (500 words max, .docx, text-only): who the players are, what the game is, what the prototype contains, and the future-state vision. It must use the provided template and keep its fixed sections, and must not contain the creator's name or any other identifying information. Judges read it as context for playing the prototype; it is not itself ranked.
A build log (markdown): a running record kept during the build, covering the decisions you lock and what happens each session. It shows the prototype was built with AI; not scored on quality.

7. WINNER SELECTION & NOTIFICATION
 

Sponsor reserves the sole right to determine the eligibility and judging methodologies for all submissions. This process may utilize expert panels, peer review, automated AI-driven analysis, or any combination thereof to ensure efficient, fair, and objective evaluation. Eligible Entries will be evaluated based on the Judging Criteria listed below by a panel of experts (“Judge(s)”) approximately during the Judging Period. Judges will select approximately twelve (12) winners across the genre and special-award categories. The Judges also have discretion to award additional discretionary Special Awards prizes based on the same criteria.

 

Potential Winners will be notified via email and/or the MHCP Discord between Judging Period and Winner Announcement.

 

Judging Process

Phase One (Eligibility + AI-Assisted Ranking): Devpost will screen for completeness (all required submission artifacts present, file requirements met). Entries will be checked against the Disqualification Criteria in Section 5. AI-assisted evaluation tools will be used to assist with preliminary ranking of submissions; however, all advancement decisions will be reviewed and confirmed by qualified human reviewers. Submissions will be screened for attempts to manipulate automated evaluation systems, including embedded instructions or prompt injection, which will result in disqualification per Section 5. The competition team will review rankings and determine which submissions advance to Phase Two.
Phase Two (Meta Judges): Meta judges will play and evaluate the shortlisted prototypes . Each judge will independently score entries. Prize winners, including special award category winners, will be selected from this evaluation. Entries will be evaluated based on the following criteria (the “Judging Criteria”)
 

Judging Criteria

Player Engagement (30%): Is it compelling? Would a player keep going and want to come back?
Playability (25%): Does it actually work? Does it respond to input, run cleanly, and complete the core loop without breaking?
Core Loop Design (20%): Is the core loop clear, coherent, and well-constructed?
Focus (15%): Is it a tight, contained single-loop experience rather than an over-scoped sprawl?
Originality (10%): Is it a fresh, creative take within the chosen genre? (Visual polish is intentionally not scored.)
 

In the event of a tie, it will be resolved by Judges' vote.
